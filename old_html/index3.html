Algorithm designer working across fine-grained & parameterized complexity, dynamic & geometric data structures and structural graph theory — and the engineering to make it all run.

My research asks which problems admit fast algorithms, then engineers the ones that do — spanning fine-grained and parameterized complexity, advanced dynamic and geometric data structures, and extremal and structural graph theory. The sharpest result so far breaks the submodular-width barrier for even-cycle enumeration: a state-of-the-art output-sensitive algorithm with Vasileios Nakos and Hung Q. Ngo (arXiv:2605.30564, submitted to SIGMOD/PODS 2027). Alongside it: skyline (Pareto-front) reporting, an extended survey of advanced data structures — van Emde Boas, Micro-Macro, external memory — and a 9.89/10 M.Sc. in the ALMA track (NKUA × NTUA) built on a mathematics degree.

On the applied side I move between C++, Python and TypeScript — currently adding Go — and I've led systems end-to-end: a machine-learning database-compression engine at the Research and Innovation Foundation of Cyprus, geometric data structures at the Athena Research Center, a blockchain node shipped to production, and five years of university teaching. Two full scholarships, technical military service, and years as a national-team sprinter round out the habit: train hard, prove things, ship.

Listing Even Cycles Faster than the Submodular-Width Barrier

Vasileios Nakos · Hung Q. Ngo · Andreas Panayi

We develop output-sensitive algorithms for listing even-cycle queries, connecting classical graph algorithms with database-theoretic enumeration — going faster than the submodular-width barrier that bounds decomposition-based approaches. Accepted to SIGMOD/PODS 2027, the flagship venue for data management. The background graph on this page is quietly running the same kind of enumeration in your browser.
